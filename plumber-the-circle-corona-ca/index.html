Verified Facts.

What We Can Confirm About The Circle

  • Grand Boulevard is a circular street around historic downtown Corona, about half a mile from the city center.
  • It is reached from the Main Street exit on State Route 91.
  • The Grand Boulevard Historic District was added to the National Register of Historic Places on July 14, 2011.
  • The street was designed by architect Hiram Clay Kellogg.
  • Reference sources describe it as a residential area.

None of these sources gives the age or plumbing of individual houses, so we make no claims about them.

General Plumbing Information.

Checking Instead Of Assuming

A street with long history invites assumptions about the pipes inside. We try not to make them. Some homes around the Circle have been remodeled, some have not, and the only way to know is to look at the pipe and test it.

A sewer camera inspection is useful when drains slow down repeatedly or there is a wet patch in the yard. It shows the inside of the line, so you know whether you need cleaning, a spot repair or nothing at all.

If your property is listed or in a historic district, ask the City of Corona whether a project needs review before you begin exterior work. Interior plumbing is a separate question, and we can talk through the scope while you check.

Unknowns.

What We Do Not Know

We do not know the age of your home, what material the supply and drain lines are, or whether past owners replaced them. We learn that from the house, not from the street name.

FAQs.

Questions From The Circle Homeowners

What is The Circle in Corona?

It is Grand Boulevard, a circular street around historic downtown, listed on the National Register as a historic district.

Does a historic district change what plumbing work I can do?

It may affect exterior changes. Ask the City of Corona about your property, and we can discuss the plumbing scope separately.

Are homes near the Circle old?

We do not know individual home ages and do not assume them. We inspect what is actually installed.

When should I get a sewer camera inspection?

Repeat clogs, gurgling or wet spots in the yard are good reasons. It shows the line instead of guessing.
