Signs Of A Slab Leak.

What A Slab Leak Actually Looks Like

A Warm Patch On The Floor

A hot water line leaking under the slab warms the concrete above it. If one spot on the floor is noticeably warmer than the rest, that is a strong sign.

Water Near The Foundation Edge

Moisture showing up along a garage wall or where the slab meets the foundation, especially with no obvious outside source, often traces back underneath the house.

A Water Bill With No Explanation

Slab leaks run continuously and quietly, so the water bill is frequently the first real clue, well before any visible sign appears.

Low Water Pressure In One Area

A slab leak can drop pressure to whichever fixtures share that line, while the rest of the house pressure stays normal.

Emergency Steps.

If Water Is Coming Up Through The Floor Right Now

Water pooling on a slab floor, a hot patch under bare feet, or a hissing sound from below all justify an immediate call. Our line runs day and night.

Close The Main

Turn the main shutoff clockwise until the flow stops. This slows the damage while you wait.

Switch Off The Heater

If the leak seems to be on the hot side, shut the water heater down once the main is closed. A heater with no supply should not keep firing.

Lift What You Can

Roll up rugs and move furniture off the wet area so the floor can breathe.

Note Where It Shows

Mark the warm or wet spot with tape. That helps the detection pass start in the right place.

Why Corona Sees These Often.

Foundation-Age Plumbing In This Market

About one in four Corona homes predates 1980 and roughly half were built in the 1980s and 1990s, an era when copper supply lines were often routed directly under the concrete slab rather than through interior walls. After thirty to sixty years underground, even good copper develops pinhole leaks from soil chemistry, minor ground movement, and simple age. Once a line fails under the slab, the water has nowhere to go but up through the concrete or sideways toward the foundation edge. That is why the two most common first signs are a warm floor spot and a rising water bill, not anything obviously wrong with a fixture.

How We Confirm It.

Finding The Leak Without Breaking Concrete

Acoustic Ground Detection

Ground microphones pick up the specific sound pressurized water makes escaping a line under concrete, narrowing the search to a small area.

Thermal Imaging

A thermal camera reads the temperature difference a hot water slab leak creates on the surface above it, often confirming the warm-spot symptom directly.

Pressure Isolation Testing

Isolating sections of the plumbing system and testing pressure narrows down which line is losing water, hot or cold side.

Video Line Inspection

Where access allows, a video camera can confirm a line's condition directly rather than relying on sound and heat alone.

You Don't Need To Know The Cause Yet.

Common Ways People Describe This Before They Know It's A Slab Leak

Most calls about this do not start with the words slab leak. They start with a warm spot that will not go away, a driveway that stays damp for no reason, or a water bill that jumped and nobody can explain why. Any of those is enough reason to have it checked. We start from the symptom you actually have and work backward to the cause, rather than needing you to diagnose it first.

While You Wait.

What To Do Before We Arrive

Know Your Main Shutoff

If the leak is significant, shutting off the main water valve stops it from getting worse while you wait, even though it means no water in the house until the repair.

Pull Up Damp Flooring If You Can

Carpet or laminate sitting on wet concrete for days raises real mold risk. Pulling back what you safely can helps it dry and gives us clearer access to check the area.

Photograph The Warm Spot Or Damp Area

A photo with a timestamp is useful both for your own insurance claim and for us to compare against once the repair is finished.

Repairs Follow Standard Code

Any slab leak repair we perform follows standard UPC plumbing code practices for the repair method used, whether that is direct access or a reroute.

Repair Options.

How A Slab Leak Actually Gets Fixed

Direct Slab Access

  • Concrete is opened at the confirmed leak point.
  • Fastest option when the leak is in one clear spot.
  • Best for a single, well-isolated leak.

Reroute Or Repipe

  • The damaged section is abandoned and a new line is run through walls or the attic instead.
  • Avoids breaking concrete entirely.
  • Often the better call on an older home with multiple aging lines.

Beyond The Living Room.

Slab Leaks Under Garages, Patios And Driveways

The slab is more than the living room floor. Supply lines can also cross under a garage, a patio or a driveway apron on their way to a hose bib or a detached space.

Signs in those areas are quieter. Look for a damp line along the base of the garage wall, a darker strip of concrete that never dries, or a hose bib with weak pressure. The same listening and thermal tools used indoors work here, and we explain which tools fit before the visit starts.

Pinpointing And Access.

How Close Can You Mark A Slab Leak, And How Big Is The Opening

The goal of detection is a marked spot, not a general area. Listening gear and thermal mapping narrow it down, and pressure isolation confirms which line is at fault.

A smaller marked area means a smaller saw cut. The size of the opening depends on pipe depth, floor covering and whether a reroute is the better path. If a previous plumber could not find the source, tell the dispatcher. A second look with different tools often changes the result. If the leak turns out to be a drain line, our camera inspection can confirm it.

FAQs.

Common Questions

Do you have to break my floor to find the leak?

No. Acoustic and thermal detection locate it first. Concrete or flooring only gets opened once the leak is confirmed and you have approved a repair path.

Is rerouting always better than direct repair?

Not always. A single isolated leak in an otherwise sound line is often fastest and cheapest to access directly. Multiple aging lines is where rerouting tends to make more sense.

Will my homeowner's insurance cover this?

Often for sudden leaks, less often for gradual ones. We provide documentation either way.

How fast does a slab leak need to be fixed?

Treat water rising through the floor as an emergency and call right away. A slow, hidden leak is less dramatic, but water sitting under flooring for weeks raises the risk of mold and floor damage. Sooner is better either way.

Can a slab leak be under my garage or patio?

Yes. Supply lines can cross under a garage, patio or driveway apron. Damp concrete along a wall, or a hose bib with weak flow, are common hints.

Is a hot spot on the floor always a slab leak?

Not always. A warm spot can also come from a heater line, a radiant system or sunlight on a window. A pressure check separates a real leak from those other causes.

My plumber could not locate the leak under the slab. What happens now?

We ask what has already been tested and then use a different mix of tools. Listening, thermal mapping and pressure isolation together often find what one method missed.

How big is the hole in the floor when a slab leak gets repaired?

It depends on how well the leak was pinpointed, the pipe depth and the floor covering. Accurate marking keeps the opening small, and a reroute avoids opening the slab at all.

Local Neighborhoods.

Where Slab Leaks Show Up In Corona

Slab leaks follow the age of the neighborhood. Older areas such as Home Gardens and the streets around the downtown Circle are where aging copper is most likely to fail, with the late-1980s tracts of Sierra del Oro close behind. South Corona around Dos Lagos is much newer, so a slab leak there is the exception. A warm floor spot or an unexplained water bill is the usual first sign anywhere.
