The Problem.

Signs You Have A Hidden Leak

The Meter Keeps Running

You shut off every fixture in the house and the water meter dial is still spinning. That points to a leak somewhere between the meter and a fixture, not a fixture itself.

A Water Bill That Makes No Sense

Usage jumps sharply with no change in how the household uses water. This is one of the most common reasons Corona homeowners call us, and it often traces back to a slab or yard line leak.

Warm Spots Or Damp Carpet

A warm patch on the floor usually means a hot water line leaking under the slab. Damp carpet or a musty smell near an exterior wall often means a supply or drain line leak inside the wall.

A Sound With Everything Off

A faint hissing or trickling sound when no fixture is running is a strong sign of a pressurized line leak somewhere in the system.

Active Leak Now.

Water Is Running Somewhere It Should Not Be

A leak that is spreading, soaking a floor or surfacing in the yard needs fast action. The goal is to stop the loss first, then find the exact spot.

Close The Main Valve

Turn the main shutoff clockwise. If the meter stops moving afterward, the leak is on your side of the meter.

Watch The Meter

Leave the valve closed for a few minutes, then open one tap. A quick read of the dial tells you whether water is still escaping.

Protect The Electrics

Keep clear of outlets and panels near wet ground. Switch off the breaker for any soaked area.

Mark And Photograph

Tape off the wet or warm spot and take photos. It gives the detection crew a head start.

Our line runs 24/7. Tell the dispatcher what you see and whether the water is indoors, in the slab or outside the house.

Why This Is Common Here.

Leak Detection In Corona's Housing Stock

About one in four Corona homes predates 1980, concentrated in the older central neighborhoods and nearby Home Gardens. Roughly half were built in the 1980s and 1990s, so a lot of the copper supply line in town is now 30 to 60 years old, and much of it runs under a concrete slab foundation. Copper that age is a common source of pinhole leaks, and once one forms under the slab, it often shows up first as a mysterious water bill rather than any visible sign. Corona's moderately hard water also speeds up mineral scale buildup inside older galvanized fittings, which is a second, unrelated way a slow leak can start at a joint that used to hold pressure fine. Homes in newer developments with PEX plumbing see far fewer of these calls, which is part of why we ask about the age and construction of your home before guessing at a cause.

How We Find It.

Detection Methods We Actually Use

Acoustic Listening Equipment

Ground microphones and acoustic listening devices pick up the sound of water escaping a pressurized line, even under concrete or several feet of soil.

Electronic Amplification

For quieter leaks, electronic amplification equipment isolates the leak sound from background noise, narrowing the search area to a specific section of pipe.

Thermal Imaging

A thermal camera can spot the temperature difference a hot water line leak creates under a slab or behind drywall, without cutting into anything first.

Video Pipe Inspection

For drain and sewer line leaks, a video camera on a fiber-optic cable shows the inside of the pipe directly, confirming the leak's exact location and the pipe's condition.

Not Sure What's Wrong Yet.

You Don't Need To Know Where The Leak Is Before You Call

Most people who call about a leak do not know where it is yet, only that something is wrong. That is normal, and it is exactly what leak detection is for. If the meter is running with every fixture off, if you do not know where the water is going, or if your bill went up and you cannot explain it, that is enough information to start. We narrow the search from there.

Before You Book.

What Leak Detection Costs, And How We Pinpoint The Spot

Most callers ask three things first. What will it cost, what gear shows up, and how close can you get to the leak. Here are straight answers to each.

Price depends on how many lines need testing, whether the leak is indoors or buried, and how much surface has to be reached. The dispatcher explains how the quote is built when you call, so the number is not a surprise on the doorstep.

The gear is the listening, thermal and camera tools described above. We use them to narrow the area, then isolate the suspect line with a pressure check. The aim is a marked location, so any digging stays small and planned.

Finding the leak and fixing it are separate steps. Once the spot is known, we can handle the repair as well, or you can review the findings before choosing. For pipes that fail again and again, see our whole-home repiping page.

Meter And Bill Check.

Confirming A Leak From The Water Meter And The Bill

A spinning meter with every fixture off is the clearest sign that water is escaping somewhere. You can check this in ten minutes before anyone visits.

Shut Everything Off

Stop the dishwasher, washer, ice maker and irrigation. Make sure nobody is running a tap or flushing a toilet.

Read The Meter

Look for the small leak indicator or the sweeping dial on the meter. Any movement while the house is idle points to a leak past the meter.

Check The Toilets

A few drops of food coloring in each tank, left alone for fifteen minutes, show whether the bowl is quietly leaking.

Compare The Bills

A bill that climbs for two cycles in a row with no change in habits deserves a closer look.

If the dial still moves after these steps, the leak is hidden. A sudden bill jump is the usual reason people call, and a detection visit is the way to find the source.

Outside The House.

Wet Yard Spots, Driveway Seepage And Buried Service Lines

Not every leak is behind a wall. Soggy ground near the front of the house, a damp strip along a driveway or water bubbling near the meter box often means a buried line.

The pipe from the meter box to the house is generally the owner's responsibility. The utility side ends at the meter. If you are unsure where the problem sits, Corona Department of Water & Power can tell you which side it falls on.

Irrigation lines can leak too. We can check whether the water loss is on the house plumbing or the sprinkler side, but sprinkler repair is not one of our listed services. When a buried line is badly worn, replacement may beat repeat repairs. Our water service line page covers that work from the meter to the house.

FAQs.

Common Questions

Do I need to know where the leak is before I call?

No. Most calls start with a high bill or a running meter and no known location. Finding it is what we do.

Will you have to cut into my walls or floors?

Not to find the leak. Acoustic and thermal equipment locates it without opening anything. Repair may require limited access once the leak is confirmed.

Do you offer free estimates?

Yes, for a standard leak detection visit. If it is an active emergency leak, we prioritize stopping the damage and confirm pricing on-site.

Can you detect a leak in a pool or spa line?

Yes, pool and spa plumbing loops use the same pressure-based detection principles as household lines.

How do I know if my water meter is showing a leak?

Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch the meter dial or leak indicator for fifteen minutes. If it keeps moving, water is escaping somewhere past the meter.

A plumber already looked and could not find my leak. Can you still help?

Yes. A missed leak is usually hidden in a spot that needs listening and thermal tools instead of a visual check. We start by learning what was already ruled out, so the search does not repeat the same steps.

Can you find a leak under a driveway or in the yard?

That is a common call. We narrow the area with listening gear and pressure testing, then mark the spot, so the opening in concrete or soil is as small as the situation allows.

Do you handle rentals, duplexes and small apartment buildings?

Yes. Single-family homes, rentals, duplexes, triplexes and townhomes all get the same process. Larger shared systems are covered on our commercial plumbing page.

Could the water loss be coming from my sprinklers?

It can be. We can tell whether the loss is on the house side or the irrigation side. The sprinkler repair itself would need an irrigation contractor.
