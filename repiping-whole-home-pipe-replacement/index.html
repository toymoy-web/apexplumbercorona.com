Signs A Repipe Makes Sense.

When Repeated Repairs Stop Being The Answer

Multiple Leaks Over A Short Period

One leak is a repair. Two or three unrelated leaks within a year or two on the same aging system is a sign the whole line is nearing end of life.

Discolored Water Throughout The House

Rust-colored water from multiple fixtures, not just one, usually means internal pipe corrosion rather than a single localized problem.

Persistently Low Pressure

Corroded galvanized pipe narrows internally over decades, reducing flow throughout the house even when no single leak is present.

Home Built Before The Mid-1980s

Homes with original galvanized or aging copper supply lines are the most common repipe candidates simply due to age.

Why This Comes Up In Corona.

A Real Number Of Homes Are Reaching This Point

A meaningful share of Corona's housing stock is now 50-plus years old, old enough that original copper and galvanized supply lines are reaching genuine end-of-life territory across the whole system, not just at one weak point. For a home that's already had two or three unrelated leak repairs, a full repipe often ends up costing less over the following several years than continuing to chase individual failures as they happen.

How Repiping Works.

PEX Vs Copper, And How It's Installed

PEX Piping

Flexible, resistant to corrosion, and generally faster to install with fewer joints, PEX is the most common modern choice for a full repipe.

Copper Piping

More rigid and traditionally trusted, copper costs more than PEX but remains a solid option, especially where local code or preference calls for it.

Minimal-Damage Access Methods

New lines are routed through existing wall and floor access points where possible, limiting how much drywall needs to be opened and patched.

Phased Scheduling

Larger homes can sometimes be repiped in phases to reduce how long water service is disrupted at once.

Repipe Vs Keep Repairing.

How We Help You Decide

A single leak on an otherwise sound system is just a repair. A pattern of failures, persistent low pressure, or discolored water from multiple fixtures is what actually justifies a repipe. We assess the whole system honestly rather than defaulting to the larger job.

During The Project.

What To Do While Water Is Shut Off

Plan Around Scheduled Outage Windows

We'll tell you which hours water will be off each day so you can plan around it rather than being caught off guard.

Keep A Water Supply On Hand

A few gallons of stored water covers drinking and basic needs during a shutoff window, especially with kids or pets in the house.

Stay Clear Of Open Wall And Floor Access Points

Areas where old pipe has been removed and new lines are being run should be treated as an active work zone until closed up.

Report Any New Leak Immediately

If you notice dripping or a new issue once water is restored each day, tell us right away rather than waiting until the next scheduled visit.

The Real Trade-Offs.

PEX Vs Copper For A Repipe

PEX

  • Lower material and labor cost.
  • Faster installation, fewer joints.
  • Highly resistant to corrosion and scale.

Copper

  • Higher upfront cost.
  • Long, proven track record.
  • Preferred in some code jurisdictions and by some buyers.

Old Pipe Types.

Galvanized, CPVC Or Copper: Which Do You Have?

Galvanized Steel

Gray, threaded pipe that rusts from the inside. Low flow, brown water and repeat leaks are typical in older homes.

CPVC

Cream or tan plastic that turns brittle. Cracks at fittings can appear without warning.

Copper

Reddish metal that can develop pinholes over time, especially where water is hard.

Knowing your material helps decide between repair and replacement. A technician can identify it on a short visit.

Service Line.

The Pipe Between The Meter And The House

The buried line from the meter to the house is a different question from the pipes inside the walls. It can leak, corrode or lose pressure.

During a whole-home assessment, we check how the line behaves. If the leak is outside, start with our leak detection page. Replacing the buried line itself is covered on our service line page.

FAQs.

Common Questions

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

It varies with home size, but many single-story homes are completed in a few days, with multi-story or larger homes taking longer.

Will I have water during the project?

Water is typically shut off for portions of each work day and restored by end of day, with the details discussed during scheduling.

Do you patch the walls afterward?

Yes, access points are patched as part of the project, though final paint matching is typically the homeowner's responsibility.

Is PEX as durable as copper?

Yes, for residential use PEX has a strong track record and is now standard in a large share of new construction.

After a few pinhole leaks, should I repair or repipe?

One leak in sound pipe is a repair. Several leaks within a short time show the pipe is failing everywhere, and that is when a repipe is worth pricing.

How do I tell if I have galvanized pipe?

Galvanized pipe is gray, magnetic and threaded at joints. Older homes may still have it in places.

Can you replace only part of the plumbing?

Yes. A phased approach can start with the worst section and continue later.

Will repiping fix low water pressure?

If pipe corrosion is the cause, yes. If the cause is a regulator or a valve, those are cheaper fixes, so we check them first.

Local Neighborhoods.

Who Needs A Repipe In Corona

Repipe candidates fall into two groups. One is the pre-1980 homes in central Corona and Home Gardens with original copper or galvanized lines. The other is the 1980s and 1990s homes, such as those in Sierra del Oro, with repeated pinhole leaks. Homes built roughly between 1978 and 1995 sometimes have polybutylene supply lines, which we check for before quoting.
