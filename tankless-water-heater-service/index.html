Signs It Needs Service.

What A Neglected Tankless Unit Looks Like

Fluctuating Water Temperature

Scale buildup on the heat exchanger causes inconsistent heating, often the first sign a descale flush is overdue.

An Error Code On The Display

Modern units display specific fault codes for flow, ignition, or venting issues rather than failing silently.

Reduced Flow Rate

Mineral scale narrowing internal passages reduces how much hot water the unit can deliver at once.

It's Been Over A Year Since The Last Flush

Manufacturers generally recommend annual descaling in hard water areas, and skipping it shortens the unit's working life.

Why This Matters More In Corona.

Hard Water And Tankless Units

Tankless units are more sensitive to mineral scale than traditional tanks because water passes through a compact heat exchanger rather than a large tank volume. Corona's moderately hard water means scale accumulates on that exchanger faster than it would in a softer-water area, which is why annual descaling matters more here and why a unit that's gone two or three years without it often shows real performance loss.

Maintenance Vs Repair.

How To Tell Which You Need

Gradual performance decline, fluctuating temperature, or a unit overdue for its annual service usually just needs a descale flush. A specific error code, complete loss of ignition, or no hot water at all points to an actual component failure that needs diagnosis beyond routine maintenance.

If You Smell Gas Near The Unit.

What To Do Before We Arrive

Leave The Area Immediately

A gas smell near a gas-fired tankless unit is a genuine safety concern. Get everyone away from the area first.

Don't Operate Switches Or Flames Nearby

Avoid light switches, appliances, or any open flame near the suspected leak until it's confirmed safe.

Shut Off The Gas Supply If You Can Do So Safely

The gas shutoff valve, if you know where it is and can reach it without re-entering a risky area, stops the supply at the source.

Call From A Safe Distance

Once away from the area, call us and, if you have any doubt about safety, your gas utility's emergency line as well.

Error Codes.

Reading An Error Code Before You Call

Most tankless units show a code when something is wrong. Write down the code and what the unit was doing when it appeared. That alone helps a technician arrive with the right parts.

A single power cycle sometimes clears a one-time fault. If the code returns, it usually points to flow, venting, ignition or scale. Repeating resets without a diagnosis can hide a real problem.

FAQs.

Common Questions

How often should a tankless unit be descaled?

Annually is the standard recommendation in a hard water area like Corona. Skipping this shortens the unit's working life and reduces performance.

Can I descale it myself?

It's possible with the right kit, but a professional flush also includes inlet screen cleaning and a check of other components most DIY kits don't cover.

Why does my tankless unit show an error code randomly?

Often flow-related, sometimes triggered by a partially clogged inlet screen or scale buildup rather than a failed component.

Is switching from tank to tankless worth it?

For many households, yes, given the continuous hot water and longer service life, though it depends on your home's gas or electrical service and actual usage pattern.

Do you work on electric tankless units too?

Yes, both gas and electric tankless systems, though the safety considerations and service steps differ between the two.

What does a tankless flush cost?

The price depends on the unit and how scaled it is. The dispatcher explains what drives the quote when you call.

Does my tankless unit still need a flush if I have a softener?

Often yes. A softener lowers scale but does not remove all minerals, so a periodic flush still helps keep the heat exchanger clean.

Why is my tankless water heater not getting hot enough?

Low flow, a clogged inlet screen, scale or a venting issue can all limit heat. A diagnostic visit finds which one is behind it.
