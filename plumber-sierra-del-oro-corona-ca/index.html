Verified Facts.

What We Can Confirm About Sierra del Oro

  • The City of Corona approved Specific Plan 85-2 for Sierra del Oro in 1985 and last amended it in February 2018.
  • The plan covers 1,374 acres at the southwestern gateway of Corona, south of Green River Road.
  • It allows single-family detached and attached homes, patio homes, town homes, flats and apartments.
  • About 595 acres are set aside for open space and parkland, including Fresno Canyon and Wardlow Wash.
  • Public listing sites place parts of the area in ZIP codes 92880 and 92882.

Those points come from the city plan and public listing sites. They describe the planned community, not the condition of any single house.

General Plumbing Information.

Who Owns The Leaking Pipe?

A neighborhood with several housing types raises a question that detached-home areas rarely do. When water shows up on a ceiling or at the base of a wall, the source may sit inside your unit, inside a shared wall, or on a line the association maintains.

Before booking work on a townhome or flat, check your association documents for who covers shared lines. Then shut off the water at the nearest valve. A unit may have its own shutoff, and a detached home will have a main valve near the front of the house or in the garage.

Leak detection helps here because it locates the source before anyone opens a wall. That evidence also makes it easier to talk with a neighbor or an association manager.

FAQs.

Questions From Sierra del Oro Homeowners

Do you work in townhomes and condos in Sierra del Oro?

Yes. Shared-wall homes are common in the area, so we ask early whether a leak could involve a line the association maintains.

Is Sierra del Oro part of Corona?

Yes. The City of Corona approved the Sierra del Oro Specific Plan, and the area sits at the southwest edge of the city.

Can you tell me how old my plumbing is?

Not from the neighborhood name. Homes within one community can differ, so we check the pipe in your own house.

What should I do while I wait for a plumber?

Shut off the water at the nearest valve, move belongings away from the wet area, and call. If water is near outlets, switch off that breaker first.
