Signs Of A Pool Leak.

How To Tell It's More Than Evaporation

The Bucket Test Confirms It

If your pool loses noticeably more water than a bucket placed on the step over the same period, that's a real leak, not just evaporation.

Wet Or Soggy Ground Near Equipment

Damp soil around the pad, filter, or pump area often points to an equipment-side or underground plumbing leak.

A Rising Water Bill With A Full Pool

If the pool auto-fill is working overtime to keep the level up, that water usage shows up on your bill even though the pool never looks obviously low.

Cracks Or Discoloration In The Shell

Visible cracks, especially near lights, skimmers, or returns, are common structural leak points worth having checked directly.

Why This Is A Real Corona Service.

A Genuine Local Pool Market, Not An Afterthought

Corona's suburban housing stock includes a substantial share of backyard pools, and multiple dedicated pool leak specialists already operate in the area, which reflects real, ongoing local demand rather than an occasional niche request. Older pools built alongside 1970s-era homes are especially prone to plumbing line leaks as the original pipe ages, the same underlying issue driving slab leak calls in the house itself. Any plumbing repair on the pool side follows standard UPC code practices, the same as house plumbing work.

How We Find It.

Detection Before Any Draining Or Digging

Pressure Testing The Plumbing Lines

Each line, skimmer, main drain, and return, is pressure tested individually to isolate which one is actually losing water.

Dye Testing At Suspected Points

A visible dye test at cracks, fittings, and light niches shows exactly where water is escaping the shell or equipment.

Equipment Pad Inspection

Pumps, filters, and heaters are checked directly, since a surprising number of suspected pool leaks turn out to be equipment-side, not the pool itself.

Electronic Leak Detection

For underground plumbing leaks between the pool and equipment pad, acoustic equipment locates the leak without excavating the entire line.

Plumbing Leak Vs Shell Leak.

Two Very Different Repairs

A leak in the underground plumbing between the pool and equipment pad is a different repair than a crack in the shell itself or a failed fitting at a skimmer or light niche. Pressure and dye testing tell us which category you're actually dealing with before we recommend anything, since the fix and cost differ significantly between the two.

FAQs.

Common Questions

How do I know if it's just evaporation?

A bucket test compares water loss in the pool against a bucket on the step over the same period. Losing meaningfully more from the pool than the bucket points to a real leak.

Do you have to drain the pool to find the leak?

Not usually. Pressure and dye testing can identify most leaks without draining, and draining is typically only needed for repairs, not detection.

Can a leak be in the equipment rather than the pool itself?

Yes, and it's common. Pumps, filters, and heaters are checked directly since equipment-side leaks are sometimes mistaken for shell or plumbing leaks.

Is a small leak worth fixing right away?

Yes. Even a small ongoing leak wastes real water over time and, if it's a plumbing leak, can erode the soil supporting equipment or nearby hardscape.
