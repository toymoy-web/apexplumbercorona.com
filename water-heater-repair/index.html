Common Failures.

What Usually Goes Wrong

No Hot Water At All

Often a failed heating element, a tripped high-limit switch, or a pilot light that will not stay lit on a gas unit.

Water Not Hot Enough

A failing thermostat or heavy sediment buildup at the bottom of the tank both reduce how well it can heat.

Tankless Error Codes

Modern tankless units display specific fault codes that point to a flow sensor, ignition, or venting issue rather than a full unit failure.

Leaking From The Tank Or Fittings

A loose fitting or a failed pressure relief valve is repairable. A crack in the tank itself is not, and means replacement.

Why This Matters In Corona.

Hard Water Speeds Up These Failures

Corona's moderately hard municipal water accelerates sediment buildup at the bottom of a tank, which is one of the most common reasons for reduced heating performance and premature element failure here. A tank that gets flushed periodically tends to need fewer repairs over its life than one that never has, which is worth asking about the next time a repair is scheduled. Every repair we perform on a tank's pressure relief valve or venting also follows standard UPC plumbing code safety requirements for that component, not just a functional fix.

Repair Or Replace.

How We Decide, Honestly

The honest answer depends on the unit's age, the specific failed part, and whether this is a first repair or a repeat. A five-year-old tank with a bad thermostat is a straightforward repair. A twelve-year-old tank with a cracked lining is not repairable at any price. We tell you which situation you are in before recommending either path.

The Trade-Off.

Repair Vs Replace, By The Numbers

Repair Usually Makes Sense When

  • The unit is under 8-10 years old.
  • This is the first significant repair.
  • The failed part is a known, stocked component.

Replacement Usually Makes Sense When

  • The tank itself is cracked or rusted through.
  • The unit is past its expected service life.
  • This is a repeat repair on the same unit.

Leaking.

Water Heater Leaking: Fitting, Valve Or Tank?

Where the water comes from decides the fix. A drip at a pipe fitting or the relief valve can often be repaired. Water seeping from the tank body or its base signals a failing tank.

An older tank with rust streaks and a pool underneath is usually at the end of its life. When that is the case, we say so and point you to our replacement page. If water is spreading, close the cold inlet valve above the unit first.

No Hot Water.

Water That Will Not Get Hot, Or Runs Out Fast

A tank that is not heating can have a tripped breaker, a failed element, a bad thermostat or a pilot or igniter problem. A tank that runs out fast may have sediment crowding out the hot water.

Before calling, check whether the breaker has tripped or the pilot is lit. If both look fine, a diagnostic visit finds the part that failed. Sediment buildup can be flushed in many cases, and hard water makes that more common here.

FAQs.

Common Questions

Is my water heater worth repairing?

Usually yes if it is under 8-10 years old and the failed part is a common component. We give you the real assessment before you decide.

Do you carry parts on the truck?

Most common failure parts, yes, which means most repairs finish in one visit.

Can a tankless unit's error code be fixed same-day?

Often yes. Many tankless faults are flow, ignition, or venting issues that are diagnosed and cleared in one visit.

What if you get there and it needs replacement instead?

We will tell you honestly and quote replacement separately. You are never charged for a repair we did not perform.

Is a leaking water heater an emergency?

If water is spreading, yes. Close the cold inlet valve above the heater and call. A small drip at a fitting is less urgent but still worth a prompt visit.

My water heater is not heating. What should I check first?

Look at the breaker for an electric unit, or the pilot and gas control for a gas unit. If those look normal, the failed part needs a diagnosis.

Is a 15-year-old tank worth repairing?

Sometimes for a small fix, but an old tank with corrosion is usually near the end. We tell you honestly which way the numbers point.

Can sediment in the tank be flushed out?

In many cases, yes. A flush can restore hot water capacity, though a heavily scaled tank may not recover fully.
