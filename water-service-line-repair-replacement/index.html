Signs Of A Service Line Leak.

When The Problem Sits Between The Meter And The House

Meter Moves With The House Shut Off

Close the main valve at the house. If the meter dial or leak indicator keeps turning, water is escaping from the buried line.

Soft Or Soggy Ground

Look for a wet patch, a sunken spot or a strip of lawn that stays green in dry weather along the path of the line.

Pressure Falls Off Quickly

Weak flow at every tap, or pressure that drops during the day, can mean a break upstream of the house.

A Bill That Keeps Climbing

Two or more cycles of rising usage with no change in habits is worth a closer look.

Why This Is Common In Corona.

Older Lines, Hard Water And Mixed Ages

Census figures put about one in four Corona homes before 1980 and roughly half in the 1980s and 1990s. Many of those homes still have the original line from the meter, now thirty to sixty years old.

Moderately hard water leaves scale inside metal pipe over time, which narrows the line and speeds up failure. Soil movement and tree roots add stress on the outside. Newer neighborhoods in South Corona are the exception, though a damaged line can happen at any age.

Who Owns The Line.

Where Your Responsibility Starts

The utility generally owns the pipe up to the meter. The pipe from the meter to your house is generally the homeowner's. Corona Department of Water & Power can tell you where the boundary sits for your address.

If water is bubbling up at or before the meter, tell the dispatcher. We help you work out whether to call the utility or have us look at it first.

How The Work Goes.

From Confirmed Leak To Restored Water

Confirm The Line

We close the house main and check the meter to confirm the leak is on the buried section.

Pinpoint The Spot

Listening gear and a line locator narrow the break to a short stretch.

Open A Small Window

We dig only where the leak or failure is found, not along the whole route.

Repair Or Replace

A short break gets a section repair. A worn line is replaced end to end.

Repair Or Replace.

Spot Repair Compared With Full Line Replacement

Spot Repair

  • Fits one clean break in an otherwise sound line.
  • Shorter job and a smaller opening.
  • Cost depends on depth and what sits above the pipe.

Line Replacement

  • Fits old, scaled or repeatedly leaking lines.
  • Replaces the weak points, not just the current one.
  • Usually priced by length and the surface it crosses.

A trenchless route is sometimes possible, depending on soil, access and the layout of the line. We explain which options fit your property after the line has been located.

Emergency Steps.

What To Do While Water Is Escaping

Close The House Main

This stops water flowing into the house. If the meter still moves, the leak is outside.

Keep Vehicles Off The Area

A weakened line under a driveway can collapse more under weight.

Photograph The Meter And The Wet Spot

Pictures help with your records and with the technician's first look.

FAQs.

Common Questions

Is the line from the meter to my house my responsibility?

Generally yes. The utility handles the pipe up to the meter, and the owner handles the pipe from the meter to the house. Corona Department of Water & Power can confirm the boundary at your address.

How do I know the leak is on the service line and not inside the house?

Close the main valve at the house and watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is outside the house on the buried line.

Can you replace a service line without digging a trench the whole way?

Sometimes. Depending on soil, access and the line layout, a trenchless method can limit digging to a start and end point. We tell you after the line is located.

Will the water be off during the work?

Yes, for part of the job. We plan the shutoff to keep it as short as the work allows and tell you the expected window in advance.

Do I need a permit to replace a service line?

Often yes. We include permit needs in the quote and handle the paperwork as part of the job.

Local Neighborhoods.

Where Buried Line Problems Show Up In Corona

Older areas such as Home Gardens and the streets near the downtown Circle are where original lines are most likely to be worn. Sierra del Oro and other late-1980s tracts are reaching the age where scale and soil stress show up. Newer parts of South Corona around Dos Lagos are less likely to have trouble, but a damaged line can happen anywhere.
