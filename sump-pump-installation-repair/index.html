Common Issues.

What Usually Brings Us Out

Pump Not Turning On

Often a failed float switch or a tripped electrical issue, both usually repairable without replacing the whole unit.

Pump Running Constantly

Can point to a stuck float switch, a check valve issue letting water flow back down, or genuinely high water intrusion needing further investigation.

Unusual Noise During Operation

Grinding or rattling often means a failing motor bearing or debris caught in the impeller.

No Pump Installed Yet

A crawlspace or low area that collects water after rain but has no pump is a common reason for a new installation request.

Why This Applies In Corona.

Low-Lying Areas And Seasonal Rain

While Corona's overall climate is dry most of the year, seasonal heavy rain can overwhelm drainage in low-lying yards, crawlspaces, and properties near natural drainage paths from the surrounding hills. A sump pump in these specific situations is a real, practical safeguard rather than an unnecessary extra, particularly for homes near the base of a slope or in older neighborhoods with less modern site grading. Installation follows standard UPC plumbing code requirements for discharge routing and backflow prevention at the discharge point.

What We Handle.

Installation And Repair Options

New Pump Installation

Sized to the actual water volume the space needs to handle, not a generic default unit.

Float Switch And Check Valve Repair

The two most common repairable failure points on an existing system.

Battery Backup Systems

A backup system keeps the pump running during a power outage, which is exactly when heavy rain and pump failure tend to coincide.

Discharge Line Routing

Repair Or Replace.

How To Tell Which You Need

A float switch or check valve issue on an otherwise working pump is a straightforward, inexpensive repair. A pump that's old, has a failing motor, or has been through multiple repairs already is usually better replaced, especially given how much is riding on it working during the one heavy rain event that actually matters.

If Water Is Actively Rising.

What To Do Before We Arrive

Move Stored Items Off The Ground

Anything stored in a crawlspace or low area should be moved up and off the ground while you wait, if it's safe to access.

Avoid Standing Water Near Electrical

Never step into standing water if there's any chance it's in contact with electrical outlets, cords, or equipment.

Check For A Manual Bypass

Some systems have a manual override to run the pump directly if the float switch has failed. Check your system's manual if you're comfortable doing so.

Document Water Levels

Photos with a visible reference point help track whether water is rising or has stabilized while you wait for service.

FAQs.

Common Questions

Do I actually need a sump pump in Corona?

Not every property does, but low-lying yards, crawlspaces, or homes near hillside drainage paths genuinely benefit from one during seasonal heavy rain.

Is a battery backup really necessary?

It depends on your risk tolerance. Backup power protects against exactly the scenario where a power outage during a storm would otherwise let the pump fail when it's needed most.

How do I know if my pump is working properly?

A quick test is pouring water into the pit to see if the float switch triggers the pump correctly. We can also test it during a service visit.

Can a pump be added where there's no existing pit?

Yes, a new pit can be installed as part of a new pump installation.

What's the difference between a primary and backup pump?

A primary pump handles routine water removal. A backup pump, often battery-powered, takes over specifically if the primary fails or loses power during a storm.
