When A Camera Inspection Helps.

The Situations Where This Matters Most

Buying An Older Home

A camera inspection before closing reveals the sewer line's real condition, root intrusion, cracks, or bellied sections that aren't visible any other way.

A Recurring Clog

If snaking clears it but it keeps coming back, a camera shows the actual cause instead of repeating the same temporary fix.

Before Landscaping Or Hardscape Work

Knowing where the line runs and its condition before pouring concrete or planting trees over it avoids expensive surprises later.

Confirming A Completed Repair

After a repair or jetting service, a follow-up inspection confirms the line is actually clear and structurally sound.

Why This Is Especially Useful In Corona.

Older Lines Make This Worth Doing Proactively

With a meaningful share of Corona's sewer laterals still original clay or cast iron from decades ago, a camera inspection is one of the few ways to actually know a line's condition before it becomes a problem. Whether you're buying a home, planning yard work, or just tired of guessing at a recurring issue, seeing the actual pipe beats guessing every time. Any repair recommendations that follow an inspection are scoped to standard UPC plumbing code practices for the pipe material and application found.

What The Inspection Shows.

What A Camera Actually Reveals

Root Intrusion

Roots growing into pipe joints, visible directly rather than inferred from symptoms.

Cracks Or Bellied Sections

A section of pipe that has sagged and collects debris shows up clearly on camera.

Pipe Material And Condition

Confirms whether the line is clay, cast iron, or PVC, and its general structural condition.

Blockage Location And Cause

Pinpoints exactly where a clog is and what's causing it, grease, roots, or debris.

Considering A Home Purchase?

A pre-purchase sewer inspection can reveal issues that affect your negotiation before you're locked into the property.

What You Get.

Recorded Footage And A Real Explanation

You receive the recorded footage along with a plain explanation of what we found, not just a pass or fail. If something needs attention, we explain the actual options rather than pushing straight to the most expensive one.

Before A Camera Inspection.

What Helps Us Get You The Clearest Picture

Locate Any Known Cleanouts

If you know where your cleanout access points are, pointing them out saves time versus us locating them.

Note Any Recent Symptoms

Recent backups, slow drains, or odors help us know what to look for during the inspection.

Have Prior Inspection Reports Ready

If a previous camera inspection or repair was done, that history helps us interpret what we see.

Ask About Access If Buying A Home

For a pre-purchase inspection, confirm with the seller or agent that cleanout access will be available during the visit.

A sewer camera inspection is a diagnostic service, not an insurance matter on its own. If it reveals damage from a specific covered event, that documentation can support a related claim.

FAQs.

Common Questions

Can I get a written report for a real estate transaction?

Yes, we can provide a formatted report with footage and findings suitable for a real estate transaction.

How long does an inspection take?

Most residential inspections take under an hour, depending on line length and access.

Do I get to keep the footage?

Yes, recorded footage is provided to you.

What if the inspection finds a problem?

We explain what we found and the realistic repair options, without pressure toward the most expensive one.

Can this be done on a septic system's connecting line too?

Yes, the line between the house and a septic tank can be inspected the same way as a city sewer connection.
