Who Needs This.

Where Backflow Prevention Applies

Homes With Irrigation Systems

Any property with a lawn sprinkler or irrigation system tied into the potable water supply typically needs a backflow prevention device.

Commercial Properties

Most commercial water connections require a backflow device, often with annual testing mandated by the local water authority.

Pool And Spa Fill Lines

A dedicated fill line for a pool or spa is a common backflow risk point that needs its own protection.

Irrigation With Fertilizer Injection

Systems that inject fertilizer or chemicals into irrigation water carry a higher backflow risk and often need a specific device type.

Why This Matters In Corona.

Cross-Connection Control Is A Real Local Requirement

Corona, like most California water agencies, enforces cross-connection control requirements to protect the municipal water supply from contamination through irrigation systems, commercial connections, and similar cross-connection points. These requirements follow standard UPC backflow prevention practices, and a device that fails its annual test and goes unaddressed can result in water service issues from the utility, which is why staying current on testing matters beyond just compliance paperwork.

Failed Test Vs New Installation.

Two Different Conversations

A device that fails its annual test is often a straightforward repair, a worn check valve or seal rather than a full replacement. A property without a device at all, or one being connected to irrigation or a commercial line for the first time, needs a new installation sized to the specific application.

Why This Isn't Just Paperwork.

What A Failed Device Actually Risks

Contaminated Water Entering The Supply

A failed backflow device can let fertilizer, pool chemicals, or non-potable water siphon back into the drinking water supply under certain pressure conditions.

Water Service Interruption

Water authorities can restrict or shut off service to a property with a known, unaddressed backflow violation.

Liability For Property Owners

Commercial property owners in particular carry real liability if a known cross-connection risk goes unaddressed.

Why Annual Testing Exists

Internal components wear out over time even without an obvious failure, which is why testing is required on a schedule rather than only after a problem appears.

Test Notice.

You Got A Backflow Test Notice. What Happens Next?

A notice from your water provider usually lists the device location and a deadline. Keep it handy when you call.

A test confirms the device holds. If it fails, repair or replacement follows, and a retest closes the loop. Missing the deadline can bring follow-up from the water provider, so earlier is easier.

Properties with several devices can often have them tested in one visit. Ask the dispatcher how multiple assemblies are scheduled.

FAQs.

Common Questions

How often does backflow testing need to happen?

Typically annually, though your specific water authority sets the exact requirement for your property.

What happens if my device fails testing?

We diagnose whether it's repairable or needs replacement, then handle the fix and retest.

Do you submit the test report for me?

Yes, where the local authority accepts direct submission, we handle that as part of the service.

Do I need a backflow device if I don't have irrigation?

Not always, but certain commercial connections, pool fill lines, and other cross-connection points may still require one. We can assess your specific situation.

Can I be fined for a lapsed test?

Some water authorities do enforce penalties or service restrictions for non-compliance, which is part of why staying current matters beyond the device itself.

I received a backflow test notice. What should I do?

Call to schedule a test before the deadline listed on the notice. Keep the notice available so the device location and account details can be matched.

Can you test several devices on one property in a single visit?

Often yes. Tell the dispatcher how many assemblies you have so the visit can be planned.

Can you remove and replace a backflow preventer?

Yes. We handle removal, replacement and retesting, and your water provider may need to see the new test report.
