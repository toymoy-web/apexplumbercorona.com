Signs Of A Sewer Line Problem.

When It's The Main Line, Not Just A Clog

Multiple Drains Backing Up Together

When the lowest drains in the house back up at the same time, especially during heavy use, that points to the main line rather than one fixture.

Recurring Clogs After Snaking

A clog that returns within weeks of clearing usually means the line itself has a problem, roots, a break, or a bellied section, not just debris.

Sewage Smell Indoors Or In The Yard

A persistent sewage odor, especially near a specific spot in the yard, often means the line is leaking underground.

Unusually Green, Soggy Patch In The Yard

A section of lawn that stays greener and wetter than the rest can be sewage leaking from a broken line feeding that soil.

Why This Is Common In Corona.

Older Clay Lines And Mature Trees

Many of Corona's older neighborhoods still run original clay or cast iron sewer laterals, and decades of established landscaping nearby means root intrusion is a real, ongoing risk for these lines. Clay pipe joints are exactly where roots find their way in, which is why a camera inspection on an older Corona home often finds root intrusion as the actual cause behind what looked like a routine clog.

How We Confirm And Fix It.

Diagnosis Before Any Digging

Video Camera Inspection

A camera run through the line shows the exact cause and location, roots, a crack, a bellied section, or a full collapse, before we recommend a repair method.

Trenchless Pipe Lining

For many cracks and root-damaged sections, a new liner is installed inside the existing pipe without digging up the yard.

Trenchless Pipe Bursting

For more severe damage, a new pipe is pulled through the path of the old one, replacing it with minimal surface disruption.

Traditional Spot Repair

For a single, accessible damaged section, direct excavation and repair is sometimes the faster and more economical choice.

Trenchless Vs Traditional.

Which Repair Method Fits Your Situation

Trenchless lining or bursting is usually the better choice when the line's overall path is sound and the damage is root intrusion or cracking along its length. Traditional excavation still makes sense for a single, clearly localized break, especially one near an accessible cleanout. The camera inspection is what actually decides this, not a default preference for either method.

The Real Trade-Offs.

Trenchless Vs Traditional Repair

Traditional Excavation

  • Lower cost for a single, accessible spot repair.
  • Straightforward for shallow, easy-to-reach damage.
  • More yard and landscaping disruption.

Trenchless Repair

  • Minimal digging, most of the yard stays untouched.
  • Better fit for root damage along the pipe's length.
  • Often faster overall once mobilized.
Pricing Factors.

What Affects Sewer Line Repair Cost

Damage Type And ExtentA short root-intrusion section costs less to address than a fully collapsed run.
Repair MethodTrenchless lining, bursting, and traditional excavation each have different equipment and labor costs.
Line Depth And AccessDeeper lines or ones under hardscape add complexity regardless of method.
Line Length AffectedRepair scope scales with how much of the line actually needs work.
PermittingSewer line work sometimes requires a permit, which we handle as part of the project.

Sewer line failure from age or root intrusion is typically a maintenance issue, not covered by standard homeowner insurance. A sudden backup that damages the home's interior may have a separate, coverable water damage component.

FAQs.

Common Questions

Do I really need a camera inspection?

For anything beyond a simple, isolated clog, yes. It is the only way to know whether you are dealing with roots, a crack, or a full collapse before committing to a repair method.

Is trenchless always available?

Not always. It depends on the line's condition, access points, and the type of damage found on camera.

How long does a trenchless repair take?

Many trenchless jobs are completed in a single day, though larger or more complex jobs can take longer.

Will my yard be torn up?

Trenchless methods leave most of the yard intact. Traditional excavation is more disruptive but sometimes still the right call for a localized repair.
